[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TITLE1001]
Digital Bugs in Cyberspace
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT1001]
The rapid progress of cyber networks
has brought great benefits to humanity.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT1002]
They are now an integral part
of our lives.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT1003]
However, that rapid progress and change
left several minor “bugs”
in the corners of the network.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT1004]
Initially, they were just meaningless data
with little impact.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT1005]
However, the “bugs” accumulated data
progressively learned over time.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT1006]
And by the time we'd noticed,
they'd taken on the appearance of insects.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT1007]
Experts theorize that their primary objectives
are proliferation and evolution.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT1008]
Despite their differing qualities,
the peculiar thing is they act as if they were
one species with a singular purpose.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT1009]
In our world, we observe a symbiotic relationship between
ants and aphids, in which ants drive away ladybugs,
the aphids' natural enemy, to obtain nectar from the aphids.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT1010]
However, ants and aphids
are different species
whose objectives don't align.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT1011]
Might it be that the “bugs” were born of a singular datum,
and only because their appearances differ
are they assumed to be different species?
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT1012]
It will take some time
to get to the bottom of this.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT1013]
In any case,
we cannot overlook the “bugs”
spreading throughout the network.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT1014]
Network administrators have come to refer to
these “defects,” who've achieved rapid growth and evolution,
as “Digital Bugs.”
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT1015]
Henceforth they've taken action to exterminate these defects.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2001]
“The ‘Digital Bugs'
are pests that devour the cyber networks.”
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2002]
The network administrators concluded and
implemented a large-scale removal plan.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2003]
The removal plan, which mainly involved network engineers,
was successful, and they eradicated the “Digital Bugs.”
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2004]
The defects were fixed, and peace was restored.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2005]
However, that peace was short-lived.
The cyber network became more vulnerable
than before the offensive was launched.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2006]
Some “Digital Bugs”
appeared to have survived
the attempts at eradication.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2007]
The engineers who suspected this conducted a thorough scan.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2008]
Naturally, they found no “Digital Bugs.”
Meanwhile, waves of interference swelled globally.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2009]
What could be the cause of these problems?
What changed so much
before and after the interference?
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2010]
The answer was clear.
It was the existence of the “Digital Bug.”
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2011]
After some deep deliberation,
the engineers decided to release the stored 
“Digital Bug” data back into the network.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2012]
This decision immediately sheds light
on the perplexing obstacle.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2013]
There was only one explanation.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2014]
The “Digital Bugs”
repaired fatal errors
and helped stabilize the network—
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2015]
It was almost as if
there were a symbiotic relationship
between humans and the “Digital Bugs.”
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2016]
By providing a place for bees to live,
humans can get their honey.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2017]
Similarly, by providing a cyber network
for “Digital Bugs,” the “defects”
are extinguished.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2018]
Since then, those that cause
noticeable disturbances are removed,
but thorough removal is avoided.
[IDS_SCENARIOGATE0004.TXT2019]
Humans have decided to co-exist
with the “Digital Bugs.”
